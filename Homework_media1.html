Fernando Mora's Response to Pluto's Cave

Reading "Plato's Cave" by Susan Sontag which focuses on the role of photography in modern society which aligns with how a perspective can change ones reality. Plato's Cave is the story about how prisoners see shadow's of objects and base their view and see it as reality, which is what Susan Sontag argues photography does the same thing in the way that the photographer is selecting the objects to display to the viewer. This creates a distorted reality because the photographer is choosing to change a viewers reality depending on the photo. By choosing what to display images can become substitutes for reality rather than representations of it. The photographer selects what objects, people, or moments to capture, and by doing so decides what the viewer is allowed to view. This creates an alternate reality because the photographer is actively shaping the viewer’s understanding of the world through a single frame. Susan also mentions how while photography can be used for good, it should also be taken with a grain of salt because it can be manipulated to portray negative perspectives to which media and people can easily consume. The media in todays society is a powerful tool that can sway opinions and change the state of the world whether that is a good thing or a bad thing. What differs from the story is that photography is not neutral or natural. Sontag suggest that intentional image-making should be brought into consideration when people often think of photos being a simple documentation of moments. Each photo is carefully chosen, directed, and edited to determine what stays in frame and what is marked up. These edits shape the meaning just as much as words would, yet this makes me realize how my own habits while shooting photos can influence others perspective on a situation or event. What I choose to capture and share can play a different meaning to the people I share. Furthermore, image-making and photography can be such a powerful tool to represent the world as it can or could exist in any moment. For example, documenting social and political issues, everyday life, personal identity, and businesses, but they can also create this false image of reality. Introducing text or illustrations to photography can help counteract false narratives by providing context and lead viewers to have a deeper meaning on said photos. Some may suggest that text can kill the speculation of an image but it can guide viewers to question what they are seeing rather than to passively trust what is being displayed. Illustration can create more subjective interpretation which makes it clear that what is being shown can be a constructed perspective rather than a reality. Additionally, I want to approach photography with more intention than just pointing and shooting at the world. I want to think about what this image can be telling viewers instead of just looking visually appealing, I would like to create a story through images and make viewers ask "whose perspective does this represent?". Sontag's "Plato's Cave" has inspired me to critically analyze images and question all sorts of media, but that I also have the power to create narratives that other people should also question and critique. This semester I will be asked to create multiple media projects using different tools and media forms which have a connection to Sontag's ideas. Such as each project does not exist in isolation but potentially builds upon one another. Being a media maker means that I have the responsibility to be aware of the messages I choose to spread and how it can impact communities and powers lives whether I intended it or not. This relates to the shadows in Plato's Cave which shaped the lives of the prisoner's reality, images can also produce a viewers outlook on topics or memory of an even.

"Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis" by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager and Saidu Bah.

After viewing the graphic novel of Motherhood in Crisis by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager, and Saidu Bah it taught how much struggle women of color face, the injustice of gender in other countries, and the effects of pandemics across communities. The first chapter we view is on the life of Memunatu who has to give birth in her village that lacks resources and cannot go to a hospital to give birth with the proper help. Instead, the village provides her with a helper who people consider a nurse but Memunatu refers to her as "Lie-Lie nurse" who provided her with incorrect instructions on how to deliver her child as it took two days for her to remove the baby from her body Her child died inside of her and she became injured with a fistula. She doesn’t want to meet or see her dead child and she lies in bed in shame and pain. She is emotionally and physically destroyed from the failed baby delivery that she is ashamed to step out to take care of herself and she constantly wets herself to which she needs diapers. Her husband blames her for the failed birth but still remains at her side. Eventually, a car with people arrived to her village. Memunatu spoke to one of the ladies that related to her fistula issue and the lady told her that she should go to their Centre so she could receive treatment. Her story provides an insight of the struggle of women in underdeveloped communities who have their own manner of tackling situations such as death. It tells how with proper intervention and help like the aid she receives at the center could have saved her first child and prevented or treated quicker the fistula she developed. Unfortunately, the center would permanently shut down after Covid-19 which was one of the only centers that treated fistulas and now a whole community lacks a valuable resource. Similar issues are represented in the story of Heela who in her community there is a high rise in child pregnancy and sexual assault cases. Most cases end up being ignored, or not much can be done as quoted by their police. Her community lacks resources and explains how abortions are a guaranteed death for the women, which often leaves young girls left with the responsivity of taking care of a child they cannot provide for and with an absent father. She received consulting and soon transferred to a Centre where she stayed with other girls and was fed three times a day, provided with medicine, and nice staff. She has a court date and is annoyed that she won't be able to continue school. She plans to name her daughter Nyah like her grandma or Saidu like her father. Eventually she gives birth via c-section and reconnects with her father. Despite the challenges, she is provided a second chance by receiving mental health and being brought to a community that can relate to her while providing human needs. Overall, Heela story once again represents the lack of resources that women and girls in Sierra don’t have access to and how abortions are viewed as a death sentence while in countries like the U.S.A can be done safely depending on the stage of pregnancy. It shows how women and girls suffer by the actions of men who are not dealt with properly and how a justice system does not do enough for its victims. Sierra Leone has a bad relationship with healthcare facilities; a lot of people died during the Ebola crisis which led to the public viewing medical facilities as a death sentence. It is common for children to die before the age of 5 and one in ten women and girls die during childbirth. This story follow Kadiatu who had 8 kids and they all died. They go to a local healer which claims she is either a witch or has blood on her hands. Her husband views her in disgust, and the village views her as a monster as they believe that she sacrificed her kids to the devil. She is pregnant again and is in a lot of pain to which she decides to go to the hospital. The people there save her life as the baby is in an abnormal position, and she will need help delivering the baby. She is able to have her child and nursed back to health. Eventually her son Abdul would become sick, and she would reach the village healer for help who just tells her that the boy is possessed by the devil. So, she decides to head back to the hospital and they save her boys' lives. The hospital would close during the covid-19 outbreak, and it brought back the fear of death that had surrounded hospitals during Ebola. Once again, the lack of information and resources can drive a community into a harsh state as badly informed people will prevent them from seeking professional help such as the fear of hospitals and the idea that death is guaranteed if you attend a hospital. Women suffer from the wrong guidance of a community such as a "healer". The final story of Tetteh is a victim of poor laws and discrimination against women. She is there for three years in a females correctional Centre and she was three months pregnant when she arrived. She was arrested due to killing her husband in self-defense because he was extremely abusive before and during her pregnancy.
